# Lining: Chapters 1–4

The lining editor contains user-created reaches, Chapter 1 CNS Soil Treatment, Chapter 2 Lining, Chapter 3 Joints, and Chapter 4 Drainage and Pressure Relief. The previous worksheet editors were removed. Section/tier observations inform the user; they do not automatically create reaches or prescribe treatment. Chapters 1, 2 and 4 can be designed in any order; unresolved quantities stay excluded. Chapter 3 first links to Chapter 2 until its lining design is completed, because joint options depend on the lining method and thickness. Missing section quantities do not block that prerequisite. CNS edits recalculate quantities without clearing other chapters' design completion. Lining edits invalidate joint completion; drainage completion is invalidated when the changed lining surfaces conflict with saved outlets. Chapter 4 can be completed before lining surfaces are known, with its outlet quantities pending. Chapter 3 and 4 completion buttons and error links scroll to and highlight unresolved cards. Reach status lists actual completed chapters rather than assuming sequential completion.

Questions appear progressively: CNS yes/no; bed and both inner sides or bed only; bed thickness and, where applicable, common side thickness; borrow area or suitable excavated CNS heaps; borrow compaction of 98% or 95%. These resolve CAW 7-1, 7-2 and 7-3. No choices are selected initially. Excavated heaps use the 95% specification. A completed chapter persists in project JSON and becomes incomplete after an answer or extent changes.

## Measurement

`canalCns.ts` constructs offset polygons at each section. The current design profile is the finished opening and remains the original excavation boundary. Chapter 2 lining and LDPE thicknesses move CNS behind those layers. Side coverage reaches the inherited bank-top height (FSD + freeboard). Inner shelves follow the design. Offsets are normal to the surface and corners use intersecting offset edges, so bed/side junctions are measured once. Each physical layer contributes extra excavation or ordinary fill replacement once.

Exact vertical-slice integration partitions the polygon against natural ground, design cutting/filling bands, existing stripping/foundation removal and hearting zones. Breakpoints include every edge intersection. Additional natural-ground removal adds excavation; CNS within planned earth fill replaces that fill; removal already measured is not billed again. Replacement is deducted from the appropriate homogeneous, hearting or casing quantity.

Cross-section areas in m² become volumes in m³ using average end area × interval length. Arbitrary reach boundaries interpolate adjoining section ground and strata thicknesses. All interior sections must have ground and design; missing data is not bridged silently. Additional excavation uses section strata mapped by order to the configured excavation bands. Without section strata it uses the configured excavation percentages; the sum must fully classify the additional cut. The deepest entered stratum continues below its entered boundary, matching the existing section model.

Invalid reaches, incomplete answers, overlapping CNS reaches, unsupported repair-mode adjustments, unsupported layer placement or insufficient classification are excluded from billing. Existing embedded blanket/filter arrangements require reconciliation before automatic CNS fill replacement; Chapter 1 does not guess their overlap. No automatic soil-suitability or CNS-thickness recommendation is made.

## Existing projects

`CanalLiningReach.cnsChapter` and `liningChapter` are optional and versioned. Reaches without the new chapters retain previous quantities, but their old editors are unavailable. “Start Chapter 1” explicitly replaces the reach's active lining calculation path while retaining earlier settings in saved data. New chapter items replace old surface/accessory billing.

## Chapter 2 questions and quantities

Begin with surface checkboxes. Multiple surfaces reveal a same-specification Yes/No question. Then show one shared specification or separate cards. Construction choices are in-situ concrete, stone slabs, PCC slabs and rubble masonry; published PCC fixing and rubble masonry items are restricted to sides. Concrete placement and the approved grade/thickness/aggregate combination resolve CAW 7-6/7/8/10/11/12/13/14/15/16/17. Fixed catalogue thicknesses cannot be changed; 7-13 asks for specified thickness. Stone slabs resolve 7-27 with thickness restricted to 25–40 mm. Masonry resolves 7-45–48 by source/specification; missing published thicknesses require entry.

PCC offers all four published lining-slab sizes (7-38/40/42/43), then asks whether slabs are manufactured or supplied, the count to manufacture, and whether lug supports are specified. For manufactured slabs, the UI suggests a starting count by dividing the selected developed lining area by one slab's face area and rounding up. The user can apply or edit this count after checking the slab layout; the software does not silently fill it or infer cut, edge, and joint details. The UI suggests size matches 7-38→7-39, 7-40→7-41 and 7-43→7-44; 7-42 has no listed paired lug. These are nonbinding suggestions: any listed lug size may be selected without a drawing-reference gate. For manufactured lug slabs, the UI suggests a count from the measured support lengths and the selected lug's length, rounding each separate line up. The user may apply or edit this estimate. The lug item codes are displayed after the support layout and slab count. Fixing is 7-28 (SQM), manufacturing 7-38/40/42/43 (NOS); lug fixing is 7-29 (RM), manufacture 7-39/41/44 (NOS). Supplied slabs do not bill manufacturing.

Concrete asks whether reinforcement is specified. Yes reveals reinforced surfaces and approved total bar-schedule kg or specified kg/m²; these resolve 7-5. Shared specifications use one total schedule quantity; area-based steel uses only selected reinforced surfaces. Steel does not increase concrete thickness. PCC items remain plain concrete. Once the lining specification cards open, LDPE Yes/No appears independently of incomplete steel or slab quantities. Yes reveals selected lining surfaces and 500/750/1000 microns resolving 7-31/32/33 (SQM). No optional answers are preselected.

## Chapter 3 joints

Start with a separate-joint-item Yes/No decision and show which selected mechanical-paver surfaces already include contraction-joint formation and PVC strips. No generates no additional joint item; it does not remove included joint work. Yes opens selected mastic and/or expansion cards. Ask selected compatible surfaces, direction, and placement. Mastic resolves 7-37 (35 mm wide × 10 mm deep); paver surfaces additionally require confirmation that the mastic is a separate scope. Expansion resolves 7-35 for 100 mm concrete, 7-36 for 150 mm concrete, and 7-34 for masonry (12 mm × 380 mm board); do not require a drawing-reference field. Unsupported board/lining combinations are not silently mapped to a nearest thickness.

For transverse joints, regular spacing requires the first absolute chainage and positive spacing; manual locations accept comma/space/newline-separated chainages within the reach. Duplicate locations count once. No reach-end joints are invented; an end counts only if it is part of the entered sequence/list. Each location uses the inherited bed width and selected developed side lengths (including shelves). Total RM is the sum of these per-location lengths. A shared end scheduled by a valid following reach belongs to that following reach, surface by surface, including a change of joint type/code at the boundary. An invalid following worksheet cannot take ownership. For longitudinal joints, enter full-reach line counts, specified intervals or approved total lengths by surface; the software does not invent their number or positions. Lengths are grouped by resolved code.

The preview unfolds bed and sides and draws transverse positions or illustrative longitudinal paths. Detailed tables explain every measured length. Joint types occupying the same transverse location/surface, or ambiguous overlapping longitudinal schedules, are excluded. Invalid Chapter 3 answers exclude only its extra joint items; valid Chapter 1/2 quantities remain active.

These joint items do not automatically add excavation or modify CNS/lining volumes. They form/fill lining gaps. The masonry board depth of 380 mm must be checked against its approved seating detail and cannot establish a soil trench by itself. Separately specified soil trench/seating excavation needs its own approved geometry and measurement. Chapter 3 shows 0 m³ *automatic* excavation rather than asserting that every possible joint detail needs no ground work.

Developed surface area is average end developed length × interval. CUM lining quantities use area × specified thickness; SQM items use area. Exact offset polygon areas separately measure physical extra cutting and fill displacement, including mitred corners and membrane thickness. The diagram shows original cutting, lining, LDPE, CNS and selected-section areas. Incomplete chapters exclude this reach's new CNS/lining items and their earthwork adjustments; old worksheets never fill in missing answers. Joints, pressure relief and paver relocations remain outside Chapter 2.

## Chapter 4 drainage and pressure relief

The first answer is whether any under-lining drainage or pressure-relief work is specified. Four illustrated choices are available after Yes: bed drains (CAW 5-8), porous CC plugs (CAW 5-9), GI relief pipes (CAW 7-19–23), and PVC weep-hole pipes (CAW 7-24). Bed drains and plugs are edited entirely in Chapter 4, including older `filterDrainReaches` records. They are not copied into the lining reach, and each CAW 5 work quantity is billed once. A record spanning multiple lining reaches shows its full editable scope and an informational quantity within the selected reach. The old bed-drain and plug form is removed from the separate Rock Toe & Surface Drainage chapter, which retains the land-side rock toe and open toe ditch.

Each CAW 7 pipe group selects lined surfaces and an approved pipe length where applicable. Regular spacing, explicit chainages, or one approved total count establish NOS. Spacing and chainages count one outlet per selected surface at every location. Duplicate pipe types at the same known chainage and surface are rejected. GI groups may separately count rock holes (CAW 7-25) and local filter pockets (CAW 7-26), each between zero and the GI pipe count. Neither is inferred automatically from pipe count. CAW 7-25 is only for canal lining laid on rock; CAW 7-26 already includes filter-pit excavation. The Chapter 4 diagrams explain parts and flow paths but do not determine sizes or spacing. Chapter answers persist with the reach; valid CAW 7 lines join the usual estimate sync. Invalid answers exclude those new lines; shared CAW 5 records keep their independent billing path.
